There's a certain type of programmer — the visionary kind. In pursuit of ideas ahead of their time, working around the limitations of existing technology, they write code so arcane and profound that only they can understand it. The tools they build look beautiful on the surface but are riddled with pitfalls, and when bugs arise they're practically impossible to debug. Yet it's precisely these people who keep creating new things that, after surviving the crucible, become one big thing after another.

Every week I'd get blocked by infra issues several times, unable to work, which was incredibly frustrating. Later I realized that besides blaming FB for this, a good half of the blame belonged to the visionary colleagues around me. The mere five or six people I worked directly with had initiated or created Infer, React, Reason, ReasonReact, BuckleScript...

So I suppose this is the price of witnessing and participating in the growth of these ideas — and realizing that these things were never as polished at the beginning as they seemed by the time everyone embraced them later. On React's 5th birthday, they replayed the video of Jordan and Tom's first public talk on React/JSX in 2013. I asked Jordan why he never went out to present again. He said, "You have no idea — when I came off stage that day, every person in the audience was pointing at me and criticizing me." React was first used internally in 2011 on the News Feed, then in 2012 at Instagram (Pete Hunt), so the timeline was actually very, very long.

Many people (myself included) often feel that some new thing XYZ is too new compared to the old stuff, too immature, the experience isn't good enough, trying to solve too many problems, the solution is overkill — and then there is no "then." But actually, what you're looking at might just be the next big thing. The big picture in these dreamers' vision is so vast — some people can see it when the picture is only half painted, while others might not get it until the whole thing is nearly complete.

If Ads/Messenger's problems hadn't been so deep, React/Reason/Flux would never have been born here.

If Facebook's problems hadn't been so deep, GraphQL/Infer/Hack/Flow/Buck would never have been born here.

It's precisely because a group of pioneers weren't afraid of how deep the pitfalls were that all these ideas became the useful tools everyone has in their hands today.

The work of dreamer-programmers is so easily underestimated, ignored, or disrespected by pragmatist-programmers — always during the process. And then after something becomes popular, it gets mythologized, as if that person had foreseen everything from the very beginning. But in reality, the dreamers' work is also built up bit by bit, iterated upon piece by piece. They need supporters and followers — their champions — to help them along the way.

Chenglou is someone who constantly swings between extreme excitement and extreme frustration. Having spent this time around him, I've started to understand where those emotions come from.

He always uses the same line to sum up his response to my complaints, grumbles, questions, and exclamations. So I'll end with that line:

**"Welcome to the producer side!"**
